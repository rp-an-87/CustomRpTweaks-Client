# CustomRpTweaks.Client

BepInEx client plugin companion of [CustomRpTweaks-Server](https://github.com/rp-an-87/CustomRpTweaks-Server) (SPT 4.1.x).

## Features

- **Hide other characters' skill notifications**: bots/NPCs level up skills and weapon masteries too (a lot, with high skill multipliers), and those notifications could show up on your screen. Only your own character can show them now. Configurable in BepInEx's config (`RPAN87.CustomRpTweaks.cfg`).
- **Skill throttle**: in raid, your PMC's skills can't level past your PMC level plus a configurable overshoot (experience past that is discarded). Throttled skills show in red on the skills screen with a tooltip line. Crafting and Hideout Management are excluded.
- **Special slots layout**: with more than 3 special slots (see the server mod's `pockets` option), the extra slots wrap onto new rows instead of overflowing. Slots per row is configurable.
- **Stash sort**: optionally keep the top N rows of the stash free when sorting; they are only used when everything else is full. Hovering the sort button shades the reserved rows.
- **Quick buy for crafts**: the flea market buy button of a hideout craft buys what the craft needs itself instead of opening a flea market search, with the same engine as the barter button below (cheapest offers first, other players' and traders'; ranked by price in roubles whatever the currency; dollar and euro offers when cheaper and you hold the money). A setting picks what it buys: everything, only what you don't hold, or only what you don't hold with your found-in-raid items not counting (the default; the craft takes items that are not found in raid first). Tools you don't have are bought too (a setting). Money, dog tags and found-in-raid requirements are not for sale and are used from the stash. Hovering the button shows what it would buy and cost, or why it can't be done; nothing is bought unless all of it can be had and paid for. Starting the craft stays your click.
- **Relaxed prone bipod deploy**: the game only deploys a bipod prone on low-poly terrain with nothing within 20 cm of the spot, ground right under the bipod and nearly level. This does the same search with looser settings (F12, "19. Aiming"): other solid surfaces count as ground, a smaller clear space, a deeper reach, the edge of a ledge (spots back toward you are tried), and steeper ground. The bipod rests on what it finds, so it may not look perfect. Anything unexpected falls back to the game's own search. With verbose logging, each attempt logs what the game's own checks found.
- **Zero from rangefinder**: while aiming through a sight, a hotkey (default Alt+Page Up) sets its zero to the distance the rangefinder attached to your weapon (a RAPTAR) shows, rounded down (or up, a setting) to a zero step the sight offers. The rangefinder must be switched on and its display in use; the handheld rangefinder is not used. It uses the game's own zeroing steps, so it only works while aiming.
- **Zero follows the loaded ammo**: the game builds every sight's zero from the weapon's default ammo. With this on (a setting), your weapon's zero is built from the round about to fire (chambered, else first in the magazine) and rebuilt while aiming whenever that round changes.
- **Finer zero steps** (a setting, with an editable list): adds 50, 100, ... 300, 333, 366, 400, every 25 m to 600, then 620 to 700 (the Extended Zero Ranges list) to every sight, inside each sight's own range, so the rangefinder zero picks closer.
- **Reset zero to minimum**: while aiming through a sight, a hotkey (default Alt+Page Down) sets its zero back to the sight's shortest step.
- **Bipod visual fix**: after a reload or weapon switch the bipod could look deployed while its real state was off (the next press then "deployed" it again). A few times a second the weapon's animation flag is compared with the real bipod state and set to match when they differ (F12 "3c", on by default). Verbose logging notes the state and flag (`Bipod:` lines).
- **Refill for the generator and the water collector**: a Refill button over a generator fuel slot (or the water collector's Water filter slot, with the same rules and the one item) that is empty or whose fuel ran out. It buys one full fuel tank (the cheapest full offer, other players' and traders', same engine as above) and puts it in the slot; the empty tank goes back to the stash. It first uses a tank already in the stash that still has fuel (Metal before Expeditionary, the fullest first). Otherwise it buys a Metal fuel tank at a normal price, else an Expeditionary one at a normal price, else (only overstock prices left) a Metal one, then an Expeditionary one at the overstock price. The button text is white, orange (overstock price) or red (nothing can be bought). Hover for what it would buy and cost.
- **Unchamber**: a hotkey (U, the same key as UI Fixes' unload, so the first press unloads the magazine and the second empties the chamber; it ignores a weapon that still has a magazine in) on a hovered weapon takes the round out of its chamber and puts it in a container, without taking the weapon apart. Where things go is the same rule as for Dismantle and Split (below).
- **Where things go** (Dismantle, Unchamber, Split): the container the item lies in, then any container its owner carries, then any other inventory open on screen (a bag, a dead body), then the other side (for your own gear in the menu the stash, for someone else's gear what you carry). In a raid there is no stash: when nothing has room, nothing is moved.
- The magazine top-up (T) and repair-with-a-kit (R) hotkeys moved to the **GetReady** plugin, which shows their hints in the tooltip block below.
- **Dismantle**: a hotkey (Alt+D) on a hovered item takes off the parts attached to it, all the way down (a scope on a mount on a rail), and puts them next to it in the container it lies in. The hovered item itself stays where it is unless a setting says otherwise. Works in the stash and, with the game's own raid rules, in a raid. A second hotkey (Ctrl+Alt+D, weapons only) does the same but leaves the vital parts (the ones in required slots, a receiver for instance) attached, as in a raid, so only the rest of the gun comes off.
- **Empty**: a hotkey (Ctrl+E) on a hovered container (backpack, rig, case, pouch) moves everything lying in it out, one item at a time, to the first place that takes it, by the same rule as Dismantle (see "Where things go"). Items that hold items move whole. What finds no place, or what the game refuses, stays, and a message says how many.
- **Context menu**: right-click entries for both. On a weapon, "Dismantle" (a submenu: dismantle all, dismantle non-vital) sits next to Disassemble and, by default, replaces it; on armor, rigs and the like it is a plain button next to Modding. "Empty" sits next to Open on any container and is greyed out when it has nothing in it. Can be turned off ("12. Context menu").
- **Build**: a *Build* entry in the right-click menu of a weapon in the stash, under Edit build, with a submenu of the builds you saved for that weapon (the game's default presets are hidden by a setting). Picking one buys the parts the stash doesn't hold, with the same quick buy engine as above (cheapest offers by price in roubles, traders' and players', any currency), and then assembles the weapon with the game's own workbench assembly; parts the build doesn't use stay in the stash. Each entry is marked "(buys N)" when parts would be bought (or "(built)" when the weapon already is that build), and hovering it shows what it would take from other weapons, buy and cost, like the quick buy tooltips. Greyed out, with the reason as tooltip, without the workbench (the condition of Edit build), in a raid or without a saved build. A setting lets it borrow non-vital parts (nothing attached, never a magazine) from your other weapons before buying. Settings in "12. Context menu".
- **Equip plate**: a hotkey (I) on a hovered armor plate in the stash puts it into the first free plate slot of the armor, rig or helmet you wear.
- **Quick buy for flea barters**: a small *Buy & fulfil* button under the price of a flea market barter offer (and an entry in the offer's item menu). It buys the items the barter asks for, the cheapest flea market offers first, other players' and traders' (ranked by price in roubles whatever the currency, so a dollar or euro offer is used when it is cheaper and you hold that money, else the next one is; a pack sold in one piece only whole), and then buys the barter with them. What it buys is a setting ("Quick buy mode"): everything the barter asks for, only what you don't hold, or only what you don't hold with your found-in-raid items not counting (so they are kept). Hovering the button shows what it would buy and cost from the current offers, and whether you hold the money. Nothing is bought unless every item can be had and you hold the money; no confirmation. Other settings ("01. Flea market"): whether the barter itself is bought too, buttons and menu entries on/off.
- **Raised price indicator**: with the server mod's trader overstock feature on, prices the server has raised for you are drawn in orange (slightly larger under items in the trader's grid) on the trader screen and in the flea market, and the trader's item tooltip shows the base price struck through before the raised one. The client works out which prices are raised from the prices it has seen and asks the server once per game session, right after the first trader offers or flea results load, for offers that were already raised when the game started. If the server mod is missing or has the feature or its `clientIndicator` option off, the client stops asking and shows nothing extra. After a flea market purchase that uses up a trader offer's whole limit, that trader's offers are reloaded so the trader screen has the new price. For a trader's barter offer the item counts it asks for are the raised price: they are drawn in the same colour on the trader screen's barter panel and, in the expanded flea market row (UI Fixes' "3/11" counts), after the slash. Colours, size and the tooltip are configurable ("03. Price indicator").
- **Split stack**: a hotkey (Ctrl+S) on a hovered stack (ammo, money, grenades, anything that stacks) splits it in half without the split dialog; the new stack goes next to it.
- **Select all of kind**: a hotkey (Ctrl+A) on hovered ammo, a throwable or a dog tag selects every item of that whole category in the container (all calibers, all grenade/flash/smoke types, both dog tag sides), using UI Fixes' multiselect. Needs UI Fixes; its own select-all key is untouched.
- **Hand in**: a "Hand in" button in the right-click menu of a stash item when a started quest still wants exactly that item (found in raid, durability and the other conditions met, the same test as the game's hand-over window); one click hands it to the quest. Same rules as the window: the item must be empty (no ammo in a magazine, nothing in a case, no mods on a weapon) and not locked. A stack bigger than the quest still needs is split first (exactly what is missing) and the new stack is handed in. When a started quest wants the item but it has items inside or is locked, the button is shown greyed out and its tooltip says why. Not in a raid.
- **Hotkey hints**: a small line below a separator at the end of an inventory item's tooltip lists the hotkeys of this mod that apply to that item right now (top up and repair from GetReady, equip plate, dismantle, unchamber), with the keys as configured. Can be turned off ("15. Hotkey hints"). With the UI Fixes mod installed, its item shortcuts that work on the hovered item (open, use, unload, top up ammo, install and uninstall mods, unpack, pin, lock and more) are listed too, with the keys set in UI Fixes ("16. UI Fixes hints": each shortcut can be switched off, and the number of hints is capped).
- **Keep walk speed while zooming**: optionally ignores the mouse-wheel walk speed change while you aim through a smooth-zoom scope and hold your scope zoom key combination (for example Alt + wheel).
- **Steady aim and holding breath**: the elite weapon draw perk (Aim Drills and weapon skills) steadies the aim for 3 seconds after you start aiming, but the game applies it even while you hold your breath, and the aim then drifts as the 3 seconds run out. With this on (default), holding breath during those seconds steadies the aim as normal, and the perk applies again if you release the breath within them.

## Build

Set `SptDir` in `CustomRpTweaks.Client.csproj.user` (the folder containing `BepInEx` and `EscapeFromTarkov_Data`), then `dotnet build`. The DLL is copied to `BepInEx/plugins/CustomRpTweaks.Client`.
